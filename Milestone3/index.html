Global Overview

The XXV Olympic Winter Games, officially known as Milano-Cortina 2026 saw more than 2,900 athletes from 93 countries compete across 17 days from February 6 to 22 2026 in front of millions of spectactors. For the first time ever, two Olympic cauldrons were simultaneously ignited, in both Milan and Cortina d'Ampezzo.

At Milano-Cortina, 116 events in 16 disciplines were hosted, a record for any Winter Games, and 7 more events than Beijing 2022. 8 brand-new events made their Olympic debut, headlined by Ski Mountaineering, the first new Winter Olympic sport in 24 years. NHL players returned to the ice for the first time since Sochi 2014, making ice hockey one of the most anticipated events of the Games.

Gender Representation

Now that we have a better understanding of the structure of the games, let's explore the athlete gender distribution!
Women were always part of the Winter Olympics. However, they were initially only allowed to compete in Figure Skating and constituted only 4.3% of the athletes at Chamonix 1924 games. It took 12 years for a second discipline to open to women with Alpine Skiing in 1936 (see Britannica).
Let's first dwell on how each country brings each gender to the games. Are some countries bringing more men or women in their delegations? Or is this evenly matched?

Conclusion

Gender representation at the Winter Olympics has come a long way, with Milano-Cortina 2026 marking a significant milestone in the journey towards equality. The pursuit of equal opportunities for all athletes regardless of gender or country of origin remains ongoing, as enshrined in the Olympic Charter, which calls on the IOC to "encourage and support the promotion of women in sport at all levels and in all structures, with a view to implementing the principle of equality of men and women."
